# Infrared, not radio: wiring Pokéwalkers into melonDS

The Pokéwalker talks to Pokémon HeartGold/SoulSilver over **infrared light using the IrDA SIR physical layer: unmodulated pulses of about 1.6 µs at a fixed 115,200 baud, 8N1, half-duplex, with every byte XORed with 0xAA** and a small Nintendo packet protocol on top ([dmitry.gr](https://dmitry.gr/?r=05.Projects&proj=28.%20pokewalker); [GBATEK](https://mgba-emu.github.io/gbatek/#dscartinfraredcartridgespicommands)). It is not radio, so an SDR has no role. The DS has no IR hardware: the transceiver and a small Renesas H8 microcontroller sit **inside the HGSS game card**, reached over the card's save-memory SPI bus, which is why the same cart works in a 3DS. To link a real walker to HGSS in melonDS, the emulator has to impersonate that in-cart IR modem and hand the bytes to an external IR transceiver. An **ESP32-S3 plus a Vishay TFBS4711 or TFDU4101 IrDA transceiver** is a sound dongle design. The software half is closer than it looks: a two-week-old Android fork, **WatermelonDS "melonDS Pokéwalker," already emulates the IR cart, targets the AYN Thor, ships a ROM-free virtual walker, and accepts raw IR bytes over TCP port 8081** ([WatermelonDS docs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/silver115/WatermelonDS/melonds-pokewalker/docs/POKEWALKER.md)). Nobody has yet published a live bridge from a physical walker to any melonDS build. The recommended path is to build the dongle, prove it first over Wi-Fi against unmodified WatermelonDS, then add a USB-C serial mode to the app as the plug-and-play product, and leave Bluetooth for last because its timing margin is thinnest.

## Light pulses at 115,200 baud put the link far outside any SDR's reach

Dmitry Grinberg sniffed a real cart-to-walker session with an IR transceiver on an STM32 board and found pulses **"consistent with SIR signaling at 115,200, 8n1"** ([dmitry.gr](https://dmitry.gr/?r=05.Projects&proj=28.%20pokewalker)). GBATEK confirms a **fixed 115,200 baud, 8N1 link that is one direction at a time ("RX gets disabled during TX")**, with packets ended by a pause on the line rather than by framing bytes ([GBATEK](https://mgba-emu.github.io/gbatek/#dscartinfraredcartridgespicommands)). Both the walker and the cart drive their transceivers from the H8/3860x's built-in IrDA serial mode, so the pulse encoding is done in hardware ([GBATEK P-Walker memory map](https://mgba-emu.github.io/gbatek/#dscartinfraredpwalkermemorymap)). The walker's **3.6864 MHz crystal is exactly 32 × 115,200**, a clock chosen for this serial rate ([GBATEK component lists](https://mgba-emu.github.io/gbatek/#dscartinfraredcomponentlists)). IrDA SIR, as a standard, uses **850–900 nm light, 3/16-bit-period pulses, and a range of roughly 0.2–2 m** ([Wikipedia: IrDA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infrared_Data_Association)). Nobody has published a measured wavelength or part number for the walker's or the HGSS cart's unmarked 7-pin transceiver, so the 850–900 nm figure comes from the standard, not from a measurement.

The arithmetic follows from those numbers. One bit lasts 8.68 µs, a "0" bit is a single flash of about 1.63 µs, "1" bits are dark, one byte takes 86.8 µs, and the largest packet (8-byte header plus 128-byte payload) takes about 11.8 ms on air. Hobbyists have learned the hard way that ordinary remote-control IR does not work. **The Flipper Zero's receiver "is a 38 kHz demodulator and only sees carrier bursts,"** while the Pokéwalker sends "plain, unmodulated light with microsecond timing" ([FlippIR-Hardware](https://github.com/Jackv-makes/FlippIR-Hardware)). The M5Stick S3's built-in receiver is likewise "functionally incapable of receiving" walker signals ([kukas.me/stickwalker](https://kukas.me/stickwalker)). Android OTG "IR" gadgets and phone IR blasters are carrier-modulated remote-control parts, and some cannot receive at all ([Irdroid](https://irdroid.com/2014/08/usb-infrared-transceiver-app-for-android/)).

**Why an SDR does not apply.** An SDR measures radio-frequency electric fields through an antenna, from kilohertz up to a few gigahertz. The Pokéwalker emits near-infrared light at roughly 300+ THz, which is "far above the tuner's frequency range" ([R. X. Seger, Medium](https://medium.com/@rxseger/receiving-ir-signals-with-rtl-sdr-dongles-5a8658a44b90)). The small IR sensor on some RTL-SDR dongles is a separate 38 kHz TV-remote receiver read through the DVB driver, not the SDR path ([same source](https://medium.com/@rxseger/receiving-ir-signals-with-rtl-sdr-dongles-5a8658a44b90)). The only way to get the light into an RTL-SDR at all would be to wire a photodiode into its direct-sampling ADC input ([rtl-sdr.com](https://www.rtl-sdr.com/?p=209)). At that point the photodiode and its amplifier are doing all the work, the result can only listen, and a cheap IrDA transceiver does the same job better and can also transmit. A HackRF transmits radio, not light. Nobody has demonstrated SDR reception of IrDA SIR, and nothing about this project needs it.

The protocol above the light is simple and fully documented. Every packet after the bare beacon has an **8-byte header (command, direction byte 0x01 from DS / 0x02 from walker, 16-bit checksum, 32-bit session ID) and at most 0x80 bytes of payload**, which matches the walker's 8+0x80-byte buffer ([dmitry.gr](https://dmitry.gr/?r=05.Projects&proj=28.%20pokewalker); [GBATEK](https://mgba-emu.github.io/gbatek/#dscartinfraredpwalkerircommands)). The walker sends a lone **0xFC beacon every few hundred milliseconds**. The master replies 0xFA with a random 32-bit value, the walker answers 0xF8 with its own, and **the session ID is the XOR of the two**. Packets without the right session ID are ignored. GBATEK gives the checksum as a folded big-endian 16-bit sum with an **initial value of 2**, which explains why Grinberg's version (starting at 0) was "almost, but not perfectly correct" ([GBATEK](https://mgba-emu.github.io/gbatek/#dscartinfraredactivitymeterircommands); [dmitry.gr](https://dmitry.gr/?r=05.Projects&proj=28.%20pokewalker)). Most of the session is raw or LZSS-compressed reads and writes of the walker's 64 KB EEPROM (0x0C/0x0E, 0x02/0x82, 0x00/0x80), plus a few semantic commands such as identity (0x20/0x22), walk start (0x5A/0x38), walk end (0x4E/0x50), ping (0x24/0x26) and disconnect (0xF4). The sources disagree on a few details (the peer-play step-2 payload size, and whether 0x2A/0x2C means "erase walker" or "unique ID read"), but none of those matter to a byte-level bridge.

That last point shapes the dongle design. The XOR, checksum and session handling run **end to end between the game and the walker**, so a bridge never has to understand them. It only has to move bytes faithfully and keep packet boundaries intact.

## The IR modem lives inside the HGSS cartridge, so melonDS must pretend to be the cart

**"The DS has no IR hardware"**, so the HGSS card carries its own transceiver, and counterfeit carts lack it ([dmitry.gr](https://dmitry.gr/?r=05.Projects&proj=28.%20pokewalker)). GBATEK documents the in-cart MCU as a forwarder on the **auxiliary SPI bus shared with the save chip**. The first byte of each SPI transaction picks the function: **0x00 passes through to the save EEPROM/FLASH, 0x01 reads a received IR packet (length byte first, zero if nothing is waiting), 0x02 transmits a packet (ended by releasing chip select), 0x03–0x06 peek and poke the cart MCU's memory, and 0x08 returns a version byte of 0xAA** ([GBATEK](https://mgba-emu.github.io/gbatek/#dscartinfraredcartridgespicommands); [PoroCYon's dumper](https://zff.dev/PoroCYon/pokewalker-rom-dumper)). The cart only reports a packet once it has fully arrived and only transmits once the whole packet is written, and IR commands must run at 1 MHz SPI with a short delay after each byte. Hands-on save dumpers confirm that on SoulSilver carts the edge connector's chip-select line goes to the small MCU, not straight to the EEPROM ([Project Pokémon forum](https://projectpokemon.org/home/forums/topic/7032-dumping-hgss-us-saves)).

This design explains the 3DS question. All IR electronics are in the card, and the console only drives the standard DS card-slot lines, which a 3DS provides in DS/DSi mode. The 3DS's own IrDA port (an NXP SC16IS750 on I²C) is a separate part that HGSS never touches ([GBATEK 3DS IR](https://mgba-emu.github.io/gbatek/#3dsi2cinfraredreceivertransmitterirda)). It also explains why ordinary flashcarts cannot link to a walker ([GBAtemp](https://gbatemp.net/threads/flashcarts-and-pokewalker-do-they-work.220396/)). The new **DS Pico IR** flashcart adds a "pokewalker compatible" transceiver for real DS hardware, but it does nothing for emulators ([RetroRGB](https://retrorgb.com/ds-pico-ir-flash-cart-for-nintendo-ds.html)). One patch, **RtcPwalker**, reroutes HGSS on a 3DS to the console's own IR port, and its author calls the result **"SLOW and UNSTABLE"** because the extra hop adds overhead ([RtcPwalker](https://github.com/francesco265/RtcPwalker)). That is an early warning that latency matters.

For the user's "no modified cart" rule, the consequence is clean. melonDS already recognizes IR carts by their game code (HGSS is IPKx/IPGx) and builds a `CartRetailIR` object around the save chip, but **upstream implements only 0x00 and 0x08, and the source carries `// TODO: emulate actual IR comm`** ([melonDS CartRetailIR.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/melonDS-emu/melonDS/master/src/NDSCart/CartRetailIR.cpp); [NDSCart.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/melonDS-emu/melonDS/master/src/NDSCart.cpp)). Filling in 0x01 and 0x02 inside the emulator replaces the cart's MCU entirely. The ROM image comes from the user's own unmodified cartridge, and the only new hardware is an IR transceiver that sits outside the handheld. The game paces its own SPI traffic with software busy-waits of about 50 µs per byte, which melonDS's synchronous SPI model already satisfies ([pokeheartgold libirc.s](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/pret/pokeheartgold/master/lib/asm/libirc.s)).

## Four melonDS IR efforts exist, and one already runs on the AYN Thor

None of the IR work is in an official melonDS or melonDS Android release. The official Android port's request for IR support, **issue #433, has been open since June 2021** ([melonDS-android #433](https://github.com/rafaelvcaetano/melonDS-android/issues/433)). Four independent efforts fill the gap, and they use two incompatible network conventions.

| Effort | Platform | How IR bytes leave the emulator | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| DaveuRrr/Ceravia melonDS-IR | Desktop (Qt) | Raw unframed TCP, UDP (ENet), or **raw serial at 115,200 8N1** via `Platform::IRSendPacket/IRReceivePacket` | Fork, "Experimental, Backup your save" ([CartRetailIR.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/DaveuRrr/melonDS-IR/master/src/NDSCart/CartRetailIR.cpp); [Platform_IR.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/DaveuRrr/melonDS-IR/master/src/frontend/qt_sdl/Platform_IR.cpp)) |
| melonDS PR #2776 (celerizer) | Upstream desktop core | TCP 127.0.0.1:43690, 8-byte records per IR byte with walker-cycle timestamps | Open, "[no merge yet]" ([PR #2776](https://github.com/melonDS-emu/melonDS/pull/2776)) |
| HachiStep bridge | Desktop | Stock melonDS 1.1's GDB stub plus a mailbox planted in game RAM | Shipped in HachiStep v0.4.0+ ([hachistep PR #15](https://github.com/lumirth/hachistep/pull/15)) |
| **WatermelonDS "melonDS Pokéwalker"** (silver115) | **Android, arm64 APKs** | `Platform::IR_*` hooks routed to **Off / Virtual / TCP (port 8081)** | Fork, last commit 2026-11-01 ([docs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/silver115/WatermelonDS/melonds-pokewalker/docs/POKEWALKER.md); [release](https://github.com/silver115/WatermelonDS/releases/tag/melonds-pokewalker-latest)) |

WatermelonDS is the one that matters for this goal. Built on rafaelvcaetano's melonDS Android, its documentation is titled **"Pokéwalker support (AYN Thor and other Android devices)"** and a commit adds Thor controls. Its `CartRetailIR` implements 0x01 and 0x02 through three hooks, `IR_Reset`, `IR_SendPacket` and `IR_ReceivePacket`. In TCP mode the handheld acts as a server on port 8081 with `TCP_NODELAY`, sends raw IR bytes, and **treats 3.5 ms of silence as the end of a received packet** (`kPacketGapUs = 3500`, "same value the real cartridge timing uses") ([PokewalkerIR.cpp](https://github.com/silver115/WatermelonDS/blob/melonds-pokewalker/app/src/main/cpp/pokewalker/PokewalkerIR.cpp)). Its test suite replays every HGSS walker flow with the game's own checks and a **100 ms reply deadline** modelled on the pokeheartgold decompilation ([docs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/silver115/WatermelonDS/melonds-pokewalker/docs/POKEWALKER.md)). The project has real caveats. It is two weeks old, its release commit is signed by an account named "claude," which suggests heavy AI assistance, and its TCP mode has only been tested against a walker emulator. A real walker reaches it today only as an imported EEPROM dump, not as a live link. **It has no USB-serial or Bluetooth mode.**

The desktop forks are useful as test benches rather than products. DaveuRrr's fork already speaks raw serial at 115,200, so a USB dongle in transparent mode should plug straight into it on a PC. Its serial path, however, busy-waits inside the SPI call for 22–25 ms per read and sleeps 10 ms before certain packets, which stalls emulation ([DaveuRrr CartRetailIR.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/DaveuRrr/melonDS-IR/master/src/NDSCart/CartRetailIR.cpp)). PR #2776's design is the better model: a non-blocking connection, a queue of up to 8 received packets, and a 16-character gap enforced between packets ([PR #2776 diff](https://patch-diff.githubusercontent.com/raw/melonDS-emu/melonDS/pull/2776.diff)). HachiStep's GDB approach is unlikely to reach Android, because nothing shows melonDS Android exposing a GDB stub. (One research note described HachiStep PR #15 as open, but the release history shows it merged on 2026-10-22.)

### A phone can already be the walker, with or without a ROM dump

The second goal, a software walker for people who do not own one, is largely solved on Android. **WatermelonDS's Virtual mode is a high-level reimplementation that "contains no Nintendo code or data," so no walker ROM is needed.** It handles the handshake, checksums, compressed EEPROM writes, pairing and strolls, keeps a real-format 64 KB EEPROM image, and takes steps from Health Connect, a Samsung Health export or manual entry. It simplifies the Poké Radar and dowsing, has no event content, and supports HGSS only ([docs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/silver115/WatermelonDS/melonds-pokewalker/docs/POKEWALKER.md)). For full fidelity, the **ATEMVEGETA fork of PocketWalker runs the original firmware on Android**, feeds the phone's accelerometer into the emulated BMA150 so the real step algorithm counts steps, runs as a screen-off foreground service, and **connects to melonDS on 127.0.0.1:8081** before trying a PC or the local network ([ATEMVEGETA android README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ATEMVEGETA/PocketWalker/main/android/README.md)). It needs the user's own `rom.bin`, and it is labelled a hardware-test build developed with AI assistance.

A third route is waiting to be built. The **lumirth/pw matching decompilation** builds the firmware from readable C with placeholder art, and its ports (Stickwalker on an ESP32-S3, Flipwalker on a Flipper Zero, pw-sim in WebAssembly) show it interoperating with real HGSS ([lumirth/pw](https://github.com/lumirth/pw); [Flipwalker](https://github.com/Jackv-makes/Flipwalker); [pw-sim](https://github.com/lumirth/pw-sim)). Nobody has shipped an Android build, and the legal section explains why that route carries more risk than the virtual walker. Nothing IR-capable exists for iOS.

## An ESP32 can do it, as long as it drives a real IrDA transceiver

The answer to the ESP32 question is yes, with one firm condition: **do not connect a plain UART pin straight to an IR LED or transceiver.** The Vishay TFDU4101 has no encoder. Its TXD pin drives the LED directly, and **a protection circuit cuts the LED if TXD stays high longer than 50 µs**. A raw UART "0" byte lasts about 78 µs and would trip it. The part also echoes its own transmissions back on RXD ([Vishay TFDU4101 datasheet](https://www.vishay.com/docs/81288/tfdu4101.pdf)). Something has to produce the 1.6 µs SIR pulses. ESP-IDF lists **`UART_MODE_IRDA`, set with `uart_set_mode()`, plus TX/RX inversion flags**, and documents it for the classic ESP32 ([ESP-IDF UART](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-idf/en/stable/esp32/api-reference/peripherals/uart.html)). Forum users report the mode works but can be finicky. Receive started working only after they set the `UART_IRDA_DPLX` bit, and one user reached a working TFDU4101 link at 5 mm to 3 m ([esp32.com](https://esp32.com/viewtopic.php?p=38000)). Whether the mode behaves identically on the ESP32-S3 is **not yet confirmed**. If it does not, the RMT peripheral can generate and timestamp microsecond pulses as a software encoder, though no walker project has documented doing so.

ESP32 hardware has already linked to HGSS. **Stickwalker runs a full walker on an M5Stack Stick S3 (ESP32-S3) using the Stick's stock IR LED to transmit and one BPV10NF photodiode to receive** ([stickwalker](https://github.com/lumirth/stickwalker)). The jarzadon123 project reached raw byte-level transmit and receive with an ESP32 and a MikroE IrDA 4 Click ([jarzadon123/pokewalker](https://github.com/jarzadon123/pokewalker)). Other microcontrollers prove the same link with proper transceivers. **Flipwalker pairs a TFBS4711 with the STM32 USART's built-in IrDA encoder and has moved Pokémon both ways with HGSS**, and the open FlippIR board shows a clean transceiver circuit with supply filtering, an LED bulk capacitor and a TX pull-down that keeps the LED dark at boot ([Flipwalker](https://github.com/Jackv-makes/Flipwalker); [FlippIR-Hardware](https://github.com/Jackv-makes/FlippIR-Hardware)). On RP2040/RP2350, **picowalker implements "IrDA over PIO"**, ends packets after 3,742 µs of idle, and offers code ready to reuse ([picowalker](https://github.com/mamba2410/picowalker); [rp2xxx_uart.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mamba2410/picowalker/main/src/drivers/ir/rp2xxx_uart.c)).

The choice between chips comes down to transports. The **ESP32-S3** has native USB, Wi-Fi and BLE in one part, and a Seeed XIAO ESP32S3 measures **21 × 17.5 mm** ([Components101](https://components101.com/development-boards/xiao-esp32-s3-sense-datasheet-pinout)). The **RP2040** has no radio but deterministic PIO timing and proven SIR code, which makes it the best fallback for a USB-only dongle. The **ESP32-C3** has fixed USB Serial/JTAG hardware with a generic Espressif VID:PID, so an Android device filter cannot tell the dongle apart from any other C3 board ([ESP-IDF C3 USB Serial/JTAG](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-idf/en/stable/esp32c3/api-guides/usb-serial-jtag-console.html)). Only the **original ESP32 supports Classic Bluetooth SPP**, and it has no native USB ([Bluepad32 FAQ](https://bluepad32.readthedocs.io/en/latest/FAQ/)).

Off-the-shelf USB IrDA adapters are a dead end on Android. MosChip MCS7780-class chips need their own driver, which lived in the Linux IrDA stack **deleted from mainline in 2018** ([kernel commit](https://lab.nexedi.cn/kirr/linux/-/commit/c6ae522e3a50fc1ec483d7f03ece9c7a25e6de95?expanded=1); [removal commit](https://upd.dev/kernel/linux/commit/d64c2a76123f)), and usb-serial-for-android does not support them. On a PC the picture is better: **PokewalkerClient reports MCS7780 and MCS7840 dongles "confirmed working" as raw 115,200 SIR serial ports** for EEPROM backup and restore ([PokewalkerClient](https://github.com/mze9412/PokewalkerClient)). That makes such a dongle a possible second reference device on the bench, but not a product.

## USB-C wins on timing and usability; Wi-Fi is the only route that ships today

The binding constraint for a transparent bridge is the game's **100 ms reply deadline**, derived by WatermelonDS from the decompiled game. That figure has not been independently verified, and the walker's own timeouts are unpublished. HachiStep's bridge survived **three injected 250 ms stalls, but only because it synthesized local `0x2E` keepalive frames at each end**. A sustained 3 s gap failed ([hachistep PR #15](https://github.com/lumirth/hachistep/pull/15)). A dumb pipe gets no such cover, so each transport's worst-case round trip must fit under 100 ms after subtracting up to about 12 ms of airtime per packet.

| Transport | Added latency per direction | User experience | App work needed | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **USB-C CDC-ACM** | About 1–2 ms (full-speed USB frames; about 1.15 ms measured on desktop ([ODrive](https://discourse.odriverobotics.com/t/decreasing-latency/1463/15))) | Plug in, tap "Open melonDS?", play. Powered by the handheld | New serial mode in the app | Most robust; best end product |
| **Wi-Fi soft-AP, TCP to port 8081** | A few ms typical, with power-save spikes of tens to hundreds of ms (estimate) | Switch to the dongle's network each session and accept "no internet." Dongle needs power | **None for WatermelonDS** | Fastest proof of concept |
| **BLE GATT** | About 8–20 ms at Android's best 11.25–15 ms interval, and possibly far worse | One "Nearby devices" prompt, no pairing | GATT client, MTU and priority handling | Thinnest margin; do last |
| **Classic SPP** | Unknown | OS pairing | RFCOMM socket | Locks you to the original ESP32 |

USB-C needs no root. **usb-serial-for-android auto-detects any CDC-ACM device since v3.5.0**, implements every driver in Java, and is MIT-licensed ([usb-serial-for-android](https://github.com/mik3y/usb-serial-for-android)). Android's device-filter mechanism shows a dialog on plug-in, and **"if users accept, your application automatically has permission to access the device until the device is disconnected"** ([Android USB host](https://developer.android.com/develop/connectivity/usb/host)). Dolphin's Android GameCube-adapter support is a shipped precedent for emulator C++ calling Kotlin `UsbManager` code through JNI, including a known replug quirk ([Dolphin guide](https://dolphin-emu.org/docs/guides/how-use-official-gc-controller-adapter-wii-u/); [GCAdapter.kt](https://github.com/dolphin-emu/dolphin/blob/master/Source/Android/app/src/main/java/org/dolphinemu/dolphinemu/utils/GCAdapter.kt)). melonDS Android already calls into Kotlin the same way for its camera ([PlatformAndroid.cpp](https://github.com/rafaelvcaetano/melonDS-android/blob/master/app/src/main/cpp/PlatformAndroid.cpp)). The costs are physical. **The AYN Thor, the Ayaneo Pocket DS and Retroid's dual-screen add-on all leave just one USB-C port**, and the add-on occupies the Retroid's port itself ([Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/ayn-thor-3588861/); [Retro Dodo](https://retrododo.com/ayaneo-pocket-ds-review/); [Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/Retroid-s-Dual-Screen-Add-On-costs-69-and-works-with-third-party-devices.1032905.0.html)). A registration takes 13–18 s, so charging during use rarely matters. No spec sheet states in words that the Thor supports USB host mode, though its USB 3.x data and Thor-specific docks strongly imply it.

Wi-Fi's advantage is that the receiving end already exists. A dongle that creates its own access point knows its single client's IP from DHCP and can connect out to port 8081 with no setup. The cost is that the handheld drops home Wi-Fi for the session, unless the app requests the dongle through `WifiNetworkSpecifier` on hardware that supports a concurrent local-only connection ([Android WifiNetworkSpecifier](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/net/wifi/WifiNetworkSpecifier)). Whether the Thor reports that support is unknown. Wi-Fi also has a framing hazard: because WatermelonDS ends a packet after 3.5 ms of silence, a dongle that forwards bytes as they trickle in, or a network that splits one packet across delayed segments, delivers half a packet and fails the checksum. The fix is in firmware. **Buffer each IR packet until the line has been idle for about 3.5 ms, then send it in one write with Nagle off and Wi-Fi power save disabled.**

Bluetooth's problem is Android's scheduler, not bandwidth (the link never exceeds about 11.5 kB/s). **`CONNECTION_PRIORITY_HIGH` maps to 11.25–15 ms intervals since Android 6**, and a Nexus 5X that asked for it still got **72.5 ms** ([AOSP commit](https://gitlab.e.foundation/e/os/android_packages_apps_bluetooth/-/commit/b67ba1ba297fd7cc962f26f79b57f844e9613d8b); [Nordic DevZone](https://devzone.nordicsemi.com/f/nordic-q-a/18579/slow-ble-throughput-with-nexus-5x)). A round trip crosses the link twice, so a sluggish vendor stack can push past 100 ms. BLE becomes viable only if the app adds a protocol-aware stall cover like HachiStep's, which turns the pipe into a proxy.

## Validating the link without a working DS

None of the plan requires a working DS. The stages below build confidence one layer at a time, and each has a clear pass criterion. Before anything writes to the walker, back up its EEPROM, because the walker holds summary data while the walked Pokémon's full record stays in the HGSS save ([Hackaday 2026-04-06](https://hackaday.com/2026/04/06/rescuing-a-pokemon-off-a-pokewalker-after-losing-the-game-cartridge)).

| Stage | Setup | What to do | Pass criterion |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0. Golden dump | Homebrew 3DS running **pwalkerHax**, which uses the 3DS's built-in IR with no cart ([pwalkerHax](https://github.com/francesco265/pwalkerHax)) | Dump the walker's ROM and 64 KB EEPROM to SD. PoroCYon's dumper with the HGSS cart in DSi mode is a second option ([dumper](https://zff.dev/PoroCYon/pokewalker-rom-dumper)) | Files saved, so later writes are reversible and later dumps can be compared |
| 1. Optical bring-up | Dongle in transparent mode on a PC serial terminal | Put the walker on its Connect screen and log incoming bytes | Repeating **0x56** bytes (0xFC XOR 0xAA) a few hundred ms apart |
| 2. Handshake from the dongle | Small PC script, or dongle "master" firmware | Send FA/0x01 with a random ID, take F8, compute the session ID, send ping 0x24 and identity read 0x20 | Valid checksummed 0xF8, 0x26 and 0x22 (0x68 bytes) replies |
| 3. Read-only EEPROM dump | Same | Loop 0x0C reads of ≤0x80 bytes over all 64 KB | **Byte-identical to the Stage 0 EEPROM**, a strong end-to-end test of the optical path and framing |
| 4. Reference masters | Dongle in transparent mode | Run **PokewalkerClient** and **pwlink**, which expect an IrDA SIR transceiver on a serial port ([PokewalkerClient](https://github.com/mze9412/PokewalkerClient); [pokestride README](https://github.com/edgarburgues/pokestride)) | Backup completes; pwlink completes an encounter |
| 5. Traffic capture | Second dongle (or passive mode) as a sniffer beside a known-good pair: 3DS pwalkerHax ↔ walker, or real HGSS cart on a 3DS ↔ walker if available | Log bytes with microsecond timestamps | Golden traces of real inter-packet gaps and reply times, settling the 3.5 ms and 100 ms questions |
| 6. Desktop melonDS end to end | DaveuRrr melonDS-IR in Serial mode, HGSS ROM dumped from the user's own cart, real walker | Register the walker, send a Pokémon on a stroll, bring it back | Full round trip with no checksum errors |
| 7. Android via Wi-Fi | Stock WatermelonDS on the Thor in TCP mode, dongle in soft-AP TCP-client mode | Repeat Stage 6 | Same, with **no app changes** |
| 8. Android via USB-C | Modified app with serial mode | Repeat Stage 6 with plug-and-play | Same; one tap to start |
| 9. Margin testing | Configurable delay injected in dongle firmware | Increase delay until registration fails | Measured timing budget for Wi-Fi and BLE decisions |

The Stage 3 comparison is a useful backstop. Grinberg confirmed that an IR dump of the EEPROM matched a direct SPI dump, so a mismatch points at the bridge, not at the walker ([dmitry.gr](https://dmitry.gr/?r=05.Projects&proj=28.%20pokewalker)). If the IR path fails completely, the M95512 EEPROM can be read over wired SPI as a last resort, but that requires opening the walker ([GBAtemp](https://gbatemp.net/threads/pokewalker-hacking.419462/)).

## Legal lines: ship your own code, never Nintendo's bytes

*This section is not legal advice.* Copyright in the walker firmware and the game will outlast everyone involved. Nintendo and Game Freak published HGSS and the Pokéwalker in 2009 (Japan) and 2010 (US/EU). In the US, works made for hire get 95 years from publication, so these expire around **2104–2105**. Japan and the EU use roughly 70 years, giving about **2079–2080**. Every byte of the walker ROM, the HGSS ROM, and the sprites and text the game uploads into the walker's EEPROM ([PokewalkerUtils](https://github.com/Pusty/PokewalkerUtils)) is protected for the foreseeable future.

Several working rules follow from that, matching the norms of the existing projects. **Never distribute the walker ROM, an HGSS ROM, or EEPROM images that contain game-uploaded art.** PokeStride states that the dumps "belong to Nintendo and are not included here" ([pokestride](https://github.com/edgarburgues/pokestride)). Any feature that needs Nintendo data should take **user-supplied dumps from hardware the user owns**, as PocketWalker-Android does with `rom.bin`. Dumping the walker ROM relies on Grinberg's buffer-overflow exploit, and whether that counts as circumventing an access control under US DMCA §1201 or EU equivalents has not been tested in court. That uncertainty is another reason to keep dumps personal.

Matching decompilations sit in a grey zone that their licence labels do not resolve. **lumirth/pw is marked CC0, but its own README limits the CC0 to the authors' own material** and refuses to distribute retail firmware, art or the Renesas compiler ([lumirth/pw](https://github.com/lumirth/pw)). A decompilation that compiles back to a byte-identical binary reproduces the structure and expression of Nintendo's program, so it is very plausibly a derivative work regardless of the label. Nintendo has so far tolerated decompilation projects such as pokeheartgold, but tolerance is not a licence. Shipping pw-derived code inside a widely distributed Android app carries more risk than using it as a research reference. The search found no Nintendo takedown aimed at any Pokéwalker repository.

The safest footing is an independent implementation built from functional facts. **WatermelonDS's Virtual mode says it is "an independent implementation and contains no Nintendo code or data"** ([docs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/silver115/WatermelonDS/melonds-pokewalker/docs/POKEWALKER.md)). That claim belongs to its developer, and the project modelled its test flows on the pokeheartgold decompilation, so it is a high-level reimplementation rather than a formally documented clean room. For your own work, keep a record that protocol behaviour came from GBATEK, Grinberg's write-up and your own traffic captures, not from copied decompiled code. Protocol facts such as command numbers, checksums and timeouts are generally treated as unprotectable ideas, and reverse engineering for interoperability has some protection in both the US and the EU. The dongle firmware and a serial transport in melonDS involve no Nintendo material at all.

Trademarks and art are a separate exposure. "Pokémon" and "Pokéwalker" are Nintendo/TPC marks. Name the hardware descriptively (an "HGSS IR link dongle"), use "compatible with Pokéwalker" only as a factual statement, keep Nintendo logos, sprites and Poké Ball imagery off the app, the board and any packaging, and ship placeholder art the way Stickwalker's release builds do ([stickwalker](https://github.com/lumirth/stickwalker)). melonDS and melonDS Android are GPL-3.0, so modified builds must publish their source ([melonDS-android](https://github.com/rafaelvcaetano/melonDS-android)).

## Recommended build plan

**Dongle hardware.** Use a **Seeed XIAO ESP32S3** (native USB-OTG, Wi-Fi, BLE, 21 × 17.5 mm) with a **Vishay TFBS4711** (TFDU4101 as the alternate) on a UART in `UART_MODE_IRDA` at 115,200 8N1. Copy FlippIR's transceiver circuit: decoupling, an RC filter on the receiver supply, an LED bulk capacitor, a **pull-down on TXD** so the LED stays dark during boot, and SD tied low or to a GPIO for idle shutdown. Give the dongle a custom USB VID:PID and product string so Android can match it. Package it as a short USB-C pigtail so the IR window can face the walker. Keep an **RP2040 variant with picowalker's PIO SIR code** ready as the fallback if S3 IrDA mode misbehaves.

**Dongle firmware, in order.** First, a **transparent mode**: SIR bytes in and out over USB CDC, with transceiver echo stripped. This makes the dongle a drop-in for PokewalkerClient, pwlink and DaveuRrr's serial mode. Second, a **framed mode** that delimits packets by about 3.5 ms of line idle, mimics the cart's "whole packet or nothing" behaviour, and sends each packet over USB with a length prefix so Android never has to rebuild packet gaps. Third, **Wi-Fi soft-AP TCP client** to the DHCP client's port 8081, with whole-packet writes, `TCP_NODELAY` and `esp_wifi_set_ps(WIFI_PS_NONE)`. Fourth, a small **master mode** for bench work (handshake, identity, EEPROM dump). BLE (Nordic UART-style service, MTU ≥ 140) comes last. Add timestamped logging and a configurable injected delay from day one for Stages 5 and 9.

**Transport order.** Bring up USB CDC on the PC first, because the bench tests need it anyway. Make **Wi-Fi into stock WatermelonDS the first Android milestone**: it proves a real walker can register and stroll with HGSS on the Thor before anyone writes app code. Ship **USB-C as the end-user transport**, since it is the only option that is plug-and-play and comfortably inside the 100 ms budget on every device. Attempt BLE only together with an app-side stall cover.

**Changes to melonDS Android.** Start from the WatermelonDS `melonds-pokewalker` branch rather than upstream, because its `CartRetailIR` 0x01/0x02 support, `Platform::IR_*` hooks, mode switch and Thor work already exist. Then:

| Change | Detail |
|---|---|
| Add `Mode::Serial` beside Off/Virtual/TCP | Kotlin owns the port through **usb-serial-for-android 3.11.0** and `SerialInputOutputManager`. Received packets go into a native ring buffer through a JNI call. `IR_ReceivePacket` drains whole packets. `IR_SendPacket` calls a static Kotlin `write()`, following the existing camera-handler and Dolphin `GCAdapter` patterns |
| Plug-and-play | Add `android.hardware.usb.host`, a `device_filter.xml` with the dongle's VID:PID and a `USB_DEVICE_ATTACHED` intent filter, and switch to Serial automatically when the dongle appears |
| Timing discipline | **Never block the emulation thread** (avoid DaveuRrr's busy-wait). Lock emulation to 1× speed while an IR session is active, because the game's deadline runs in emulated time and the walker's in real time |
| Diagnostics | Add a packet log with XOR-0xAA decoding, like DaveuRrr's settings dialog |
| Optional | `WifiNetworkSpecifier` request for the dongle's soft-AP; later a BLE mode with a protocol-aware stall cover |

**Open questions to verify early.** Does `UART_MODE_IRDA` work on the ESP32-S3, including receive with the duplex bit? Does the Thor's USB-C port act as host for a CDC device (a five-minute test with a CDC echo sketch)? Are the 100 ms game deadline and 3.5 ms gap right, and what are the walker's own timeouts (Stages 5 and 9 answer both)? Does WatermelonDS's TCP mode accept a hardware peer, given it has only been tested with picowalker-sdl-tcp? What BLE interval does the Thor actually grant, and does it support concurrent local-only Wi-Fi? Does the Retroid add-on's passthrough port carry data? What range and angle does a TFBS4711 achieve against the walker's unidentified transceiver?

**Projects to reuse or contribute to.** Contribute the serial mode to **silver115/WatermelonDS** and offer it to **melonDS-android issue #433**. Comment on **melonDS PR #2776** so the upstream IR seam supports hardware transports as well as emulator links. Reuse **usb-serial-for-android**, Dolphin's **GCAdapter** pattern, **FlippIR-Hardware**'s transceiver circuit, **picowalker**'s PIO SIR and idle timing, and **Flipwalker**'s USART IrDA setup. Use **PokewalkerClient**, **pwlink**, **pwalkerHax** and **PoroCYon's dumper** as bench references, and **DaveuRrr/melonDS-IR** as the desktop end-to-end harness. For walkerless users, point to **WatermelonDS Virtual** and **PocketWalker-Android**. Treat **GBATEK** and **Grinberg's article** as the specification, and lumirth/pw as reference reading only.

## Conclusion

The hard parts of this problem were solved years ago in pieces. Grinberg and GBATEK documented the link down to the checksum seed, hobbyists proved stock IrDA transceivers can speak it from STM32, RP2040 and ESP32 chips, and in the last two months an Android fork appeared that emulates the IR cart on exactly the target handheld. What remains is a small, well-bounded integration: a USB-C dongle that behaves like the cart's IR modem and a serial mode that feeds it into WatermelonDS. Choosing the transport is really a question of latency, because a transparent pipe has no slack against the game's roughly 100 ms deadline. That favours USB over the wireless options.

The most consequential design choice is to frame packets in the dongle rather than in the app. The real cart buffers whole packets before the game sees them, so a dongle that does the same makes every transport, whether USB, Wi-Fi or eventually BLE, insensitive to jitter. Measuring the real timing margins with a sniffer and injected delays turns the remaining uncertainty into numbers before any user depends on them. Done that way, the same hardware serves owners of real walkers, while the existing virtual walker already covers people who never owned one, and neither path needs to ship a single byte of Nintendo's code.
